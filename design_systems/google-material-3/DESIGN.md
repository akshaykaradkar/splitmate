# Google Material 3 Design System

## Overview

Material Design 3 (GM3) is Google’s signature design system, engineered to build high-quality, expressive, and accessible digital experiences across Android, iOS, Flutter, and the web. GM3 establishes a cohesive brand identity that feels human, adaptive, and highly polished, allowing design and engineering teams to work with maximum velocity.

With the introduction of **GM3 Aurora \& AI Components**, the system has evolved into the AI era. This update introduces **energy** as a core visual metaphor to represent the underlying intelligence powering Google's AI products. Together with expressive layouts, advanced color algorithms, and dynamic components, GM3 empowers makers to craft responsive, collaborative interfaces where humans and AI can seamlessly interact.

### Core Visual Principles

*   **Deeply Human \& Cohesive:** Interfaces leverage warm tones, organic motion physics, and polished geometric styling that unify the brand across software, hardware, and creative assets.
*   **Adaptive by Default:** Layouts and components dynamically scale and morph across a range of form factors—from compact watch faces to tablets, desktops, and spatial computing environments (such as Android XR).
*   **Accessible-by-Default:** GM3 is engineered to exceed global accessibility requirements (including GAR 2024). All color pairings, text scales, and touch targets are algorithmically paired to guarantee WCAG compliance and standard contrast.
*   **Material Glass:** For Apple platforms, GM3 iOS 26 incorporates **Liquid Glass**, a signature transparent glass style tailored to Google's brand voice. Glass effects are selectively applied to floating components in the functional layer (e.g., app bars, sheets, dialogs) while standard flat components remain in the content layer below.

---

## Colors

The Google Material 3 color system is rooted in high-contrast neutrals and dynamic accent colors that automatically scale across light, dark, and user-customized dynamic color schemes. Color roles act as a "paint-by-number" guide, ensuring accessibility and visual hierarchy regardless of the active color scheme or contrast setting.

### Accent Color Roles

Accent colors are applied based on importance and needed emphasis:

*   **Primary:** Used for the most prominent actions and elements needing maximum emphasis (e.g., Floating Action Buttons (FABs), high-emphasis buttons, active states).
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-primary}` (fill/stroke), `{colors.sys-color-on-primary}` (foreground text/icons on primary backgrounds).
*   **Secondary:** Used for utilitarian elements that do not require immediate attention (e.g., filter chips, selected navigation tabs, dismissive actions).
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-secondary}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-secondary}`.
*   **Tertiary:** Used for contrasting accents that balance primary and secondary elements, or to bring heightened attention to a specific feature (e.g., badges, notification indicators, active input field states).
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-tertiary}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-tertiary}`.

### Container \& Boundary Roles

Containers hold nested elements, while outline roles define boundaries:

*   **Containers:** Used as fill colors for card-like components, floating sheets, or buttons. Text and icons must use the matching `on-container` color.
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-primary-container}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-primary-container}`, `{colors.sys-color-secondary-container}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-secondary-container}`, `{colors.sys-color-tertiary-container}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-tertiary-container}`.
*   **Outline:** Subtle boundary colors to define element outlines (e.g., text field borders).
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-outline}` (for important boundaries requiring 3:1 contrast), `{colors.sys-color-outline-variant}` (for dividers or decorative borders).

### Surface \& Surface Container Roles

To establish nesting and structural hierarchy, GM3 utilizes five surface container tones (which deprecate legacy surface elevation overlay tints):

*   **Surface:** The default color for the background area of screens.
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-surface}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-surface}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-surface-variant}` (for lower-emphasis body text/metadata).
*   **Surface Container Scale:** Tonal container options ordered by visual weight:
    *   `{colors.sys-color-surface-container-lowest}`: Lowest emphasis container.
    *   `{colors.sys-color-surface-container-low}`: Low emphasis container.
    *   `{colors.sys-color-surface-container}`: Default container color (e.g., for navigation and cards).
    *   `{colors.sys-color-surface-container-high}`: High emphasis container.
    *   `{colors.sys-color-surface-container-highest}`: Highest emphasis container.
*   **Bright \& Dim Surfaces:** Keep their relative brightness across both light and dark themes, enabling customized visual hierarchy.
    *   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-surface-bright}`, `{colors.sys-color-surface-dim}`.

### Fixed Accent Colors

These accent colors do not change tone between light and dark themes. They are used for elements that require consistent, high-contrast visibility across any backdrop:

*   *Tokens:* `{colors.sys-color-primary-fixed}`, `{colors.sys-color-primary-fixed-dim}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-primary-fixed}`, `{colors.sys-color-on-primary-fixed-variant}` (and corresponding secondary/tertiary variants).

### Generative AI \& Aurora Spectrum

AI-powered operations are represented by dynamic, moving gradients derived from a product’s existing primary color:

*   **One-Color (1C) Energy:** Derives **middle-energy** and **end-energy** colors from the theme to create a smooth, perceptual transition across light and dark modes. It is used to show continuous AI system activity (anticipating, receiving, processing).
*   **Four-Color (4C) Brand Gradient:** Reserved for signature Google AI moments (e.g., Gemini Voice Live). It utilizes Google’s iconic four brand colors with a soft blur to prevent visual banding.
*   **Custom AI Roles:** AI Main, AI Analog, AI Analog Variant, AI Complement, AI Complement Variant, and AI Muted.

## Typography

Typography in GM3 defines a clear page hierarchy across five functional roles. Each role is divided into large, medium, and small sizes to accommodate different reading experiences.

### Functional Type Scales

*   **Display:** Reserved for short, high-impact text or numbers. Works best on expanded screens.
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.display-large}`, `{typography.display-medium}`, `{typography.display-small}`.
*   **Headline:** Ideal for high-emphasis titles on smaller screens, marking primary passages of text or regions.
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.headline-large}`, `{typography.headline-medium}`, `{typography.headline-small}`.
*   **Title:** For medium-emphasis text that remains relatively short, such as card headers or list sections.
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.title-large}`, `{typography.title-medium}`, `{typography.title-small}`.
*   **Body:** Designed for longer passages of text. Utilizes optical sizing to maximize legibility.
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.body-large}`, `{typography.body-medium}`, `{typography.body-small}`.
*   **Label:** Small, utilitarian styles used for components (e.g., text inside a button) or small metadata (e.g., image captions).
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.label-large}`, `{typography.label-medium}`, `{typography.label-small}`.

### Variable Fonts \& Emphasized Styles

GM3 heavily leverages **Google Sans Flex (GSF)**, utilizing multiple axes—including weight (`wght`), width (`wdth`), optical sizing (`opsz`), and roundness (`ROND`)—to construct the type scale.

*   **Baseline vs. Emphasized:** Emphasized styles have increased weight and roundness compared to baseline. Swap baseline tokens for emphasized tokens on standalone text (e.g., unread messages, active tabs, badges, action buttons) to draw attention.
    *   *Tokens:* `{typography.emphasized-body-large}`, `{typography.emphasized-headline-medium}`, `{typography.emphasized-label-large}`.
*   **Editorial Treatments:** Bespoke, type-driven hero moments (e.g., photo album covers or personalized header text). They leverage custom variable axis settings to convey specific emotions. Enable automatic optical sizing so the typeface always displays at its best.

### Typesetting \& Accessibility Guidelines

*   **Bounding Box vs. Baseline:** Web and iOS rely on bounding boxes (center-aligned with half-leading spacing). Android uses the invisible text baseline to measure precise vertical offsets.
*   **Tabular Numbers:** For tables, list numbers, and clocks where values update frequently, use monospaced tabular numerals to maintain proper optical alignment.
*   **Contrast Ratios:** Text must have a 4.5:1 minimum contrast ratio with the background (3:1 for large text).
*   **Hyperlinks:** Always underline hyperlinked body text and style it using `{colors.sys-color-primary}` or `{colors.sys-color-tertiary}`.

## Layout

GM3 Layout utilizes a robust, column-based layout grid combined with safety regions and rulers to create consistent adaptive structures across all platforms.

### Layout Scaffold Elements

The scaffold provides a standard platform to organize screen elements:

*   **Bars:** Frame the page to assist in navigation (e.g., app bars, bottom navigation). They can span one or multiple content panes.
*   **Rails:** The perimeter space surrounding content panes. Holds navigation rails, toolbars, chat input fields, and FABs.
*   **Panes:** Contain all page content. Layouts display 1 to 3 panes (fixed, flexible, floating, or semi-permanent) depending on available screen width.
*   **Rulers:** Global alignment lines used to consistently position elements:
    *   *Bar \& Safety Rulers:* Reserve space for device notches, system status bars, and gestures.
    *   *Title Rulers:* Align page titles and back buttons in app bars.
    *   *Content Rulers:* Standardize where primary text, cards, and carousels are anchored.

### Breakpoints System

Formerly known as window size classes, breakpoints dictate how layouts reorganize and how many panes are visible:

1.  **Compact (Widths \< 600dp / Phones):** Single-pane layout. Typically uses bottom navigation bars, bottom sheets, and full-screen dialogs.
2.  **Medium (Widths 600dp – 839dp / Tablets or Foldables in portrait):** Typically 1-pane or split-pane layout. Uses navigation rails.
3.  **Expanded (Widths 840dp – 1199dp / Landscape Tablets or Desktop):** 2-pane layout (primary pane + supporting pane). Uses navigation drawers.
4.  **Large (Widths 1200dp – 1599dp) \& Extra-Large (Widths \>= 1600dp / Ultra-wide monitors):** 3-pane layout. Spacing can widen to create elegant negative space.

### Spacing Scale (8dp Scale)

GM3’s spacing system is linear and built on an 8dp (8px) scale where `{measurements.measurement-space100}` equals 8dp:

*   `{measurements.measurement-space0}`: 0
*   `{measurements.measurement-space25}`: 2px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space50}`: 4px (Used for half-steps)
*   `{measurements.measurement-space75}`: 6px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space100}`: 8px (Base)
*   `{measurements.measurement-space125}`: 10px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space150}`: 12px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space175}`: 14px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space200}`: 16px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space250}`: 20px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space300}`: 24px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space400}`: 32px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space450}`: 36px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space500}`: 40px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space600}`: 48px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space700}`: 56px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space800}`: 64px
*   `{measurements.measurement-space900}`: 72px

### Spacing Categories

*   **Padding:** Spacing inside an element, buffering the content from the container edge.
*   **Gaps:** Space between elements within a container (horizontal gaps between adjacent items; vertical gaps between stacked items).
*   **Margins:** Spacing outside an element, buffering it from the parent container or screen edge. In components, prefer padding and gaps over margins.

### Bidirectionality \& Spacing RTL

For right-to-left (RTL) languages, layouts and components swap completely. Positional tokens utilize **leading** and **trailing** edges rather than left and right, ensuring a seamless vertical and horizontal mirror effect.

### Information Density \& Component Scaling

To accommodate data-heavy views (like data tables or long forms), vertical padding can be reduced by 4dp increments (the negative component density scale: -1, -2, -3). **Never apply dense scaling by default.** Keep targets at a minimum of 48x48 CSS pixels for accessibility, allowing people to opt in.

## Elevation \& Depth

GM3 utilizes a z-axis elevation scale (Levels 0 through 5) measured in density-independent pixels (dp) to establish tactile layers. Unlike older versions of Material, GM3 relies primarily on **tonal differences** (varying Surface Container colors) rather than heavy drop shadows to indicate visual boundaries.

### Depicting Elevation

*   **Tonal Separation:** Overlapping containment areas or scrolling areas use distinct Surface Container roles (e.g., surface body area with a surface container navigation rail) to show depth naturally.
*   **Visible Shadows:** Shadows are applied selectively to protect elements from busy backgrounds or to encourage user interaction. As a component is hovered or focused, it temporarily raises its elevation by one level, increasing the shadow's blur and softness.
*   **Scrims:** Modals, expanded drawers, and dialogs are isolated from content using a dark overlay in `{colors.sys-color-scrim}` at 32% opacity.

### Component Resting Elevation Scale

*   **Level 5 (12dp):** *Not assigned as resting.* (Used for active pressed FAB states).
*   **Level 4 (8dp):** *Not assigned as resting.* (Used for active hovered FAB states).
*   **Level 3 (6dp):** Date pickers, Modal dialogs, Floating Action Buttons (FABs), Extended FABs, Search app bars, Time pickers.
*   **Level 2 (3dp):** Scrolled app bars, dropdown menus, navigation bars, rich tooltips, toolbars.
*   **Level 1 (1dp):** Banners, modal bottom sheets, elevated buttons, elevated cards, elevated chips, modal navigation drawers.
*   **Level 0 (0dp):** Unscrolled app bars, flat filled/tonal/outlined buttons, button groups, carousels, flat chips, full-screen dialogs, navigation rails, sliders, tabs, lists.

## Shapes

Visual elements in GM3 are shaped using an organized scale of 10 size-based corner styles. Rounded corners enhance modern expression and are designed to morph smoothly during interactions.

### Corner Radius Scale

1.  **None:** `{shapes.none}` (0px)
2.  **Extra Small:** `{shapes.extra-small}` (4px) — Used for extra-small top corners of text fields.
3.  **Small:** `{shapes.small}` (8px) — Used for text fields and checkbox indicators.
4.  **Medium:** `{shapes.medium}` (12px) — Default for standard buttons, cards, and small menus.
5.  **Large:** `{shapes.large}` (16px) — Used for carousels, dialogs, and large cards.
6.  **Large Increased:** `{shapes.large-increased}` (20px).
7.  **Extra Large:** `{shapes.extra-large}` (28px).
8.  **Extra Large Increased:** `{shapes.extra-large-increased}` (32px).
9.  **Extra Extra Large:** `{shapes.extra-extra-large}` (48px) — Used for large sheets, avatar frames, or spatial panels.
10. **Full:** `{shapes.full}` (50% / fully rounded) — Used for badges, buttons, icon buttons, sliders, switches, and search app bars.

### Symmetry, Tension, \& Customization

*   **Symmetry \& Asymmetrical Shapes:** While most containers are symmetric, asymmetrical shapes are used in grouped items (like menus, split buttons, or AI suggestion chips) where inner corners are sharp and outer corners are rounded. Positional tokens like `{shapes.extra-large-top}` or `{shapes.large-end}` control these offsets.
*   **Optical Roundness:** When nesting rounded components (e.g., an avatar inside a card), always adjust the inner component's corner radius so they look balanced. Calculate this via: `Outer Radius - Padding = Inner Radius`.
*   **Tension:** Inject creative tension by pairing highly rounded elements with sharp, square shapes on the same screen to draw visual attention to hero elements or selected states.
*   **Shape Morphing:** Under the hood, shape morphing connects function and feeling. Buttons, button groups, and loading indicators morph to more square shapes when selected or pressed, utilizing the dynamic *expressive motion scheme*.

## Components

The following guidelines dictate the visual rendering, states, sizing, and styling rules for core and emerging AI components in GM3.

### 1. Buttons

*   **Visual Styles:** Elevated, Filled, Tonal, Outlined, and Text.
*   **Emphasis Placement:** Only use one high-emphasis button (Filled) per screen for the primary action. Use Outlined or Tonal buttons for secondary choices, and Text buttons for supplementary, non-disruptive actions (like "Learn more").
*   **Label Styling:** Keep labels very brief (1–3 words) and set in sentence case. Never truncate or wrap text.
*   **States \& Pressed Morph:** Buttons are fully rounded (`{shapes.full}`) at rest. When pressed, they morph into square shapes (e.g., Small buttons morph to `{shapes.small}` 8px; Medium to `{shapes.medium}` 12px; Large to `{shapes.large}` 16px).
*   **Target Size:** Must maintain a minimum touch target area of 48x48dp (44x44pt on iOS).
*   **iOS Specifications:** Small buttons on iOS are 44dp tall (Android is 40dp) to match native iOS cell heights. iOS does not support hover and focus states, and applies Liquid Glass effects to buttons residing in the functional app layer.

### 2. Chips

*   **Types:** Assist, Filter, Input, and Suggestion.
*   **AI Assist Chips:** Used to start AI actions (e.g., "Generate summary"). Features an AI spark icon on the leading edge. In its active generation state, the stroke is removed, the label changes to "Generating", and it fills with an animated AI gradient. Height is 32dp (mobile) / 36dp (desktop).
*   **AI Suggestion Chips:** Offer prompt ideas. They have a unique asymmetrical chat-bubble shape: the top trailing corner has a 4dp radius (`{shapes.extra-small}`), while all other corners have a half-height radius (16dp on mobile, 18dp on desktop).

### 3. Lists

*   **Structure:** Stacked rows of text, leading media (avatar/icon), and trailing actions.
*   **Visual Dividers:** Divided using `{colors.sys-color-outline-variant}` at 1px height.
*   **iOS Adjustments:** For list-based actions on iOS, use standard list cells rather than buttons.

### 4. Tooltips

*   **Plain Tooltips:** Used for concise, one-line icon labels.
*   **Rich Tooltips:** Used for detailed, multi-line explanations. Resting elevation level is Level 2 (3dp), styled with Surface Container background colors.

### 5. Checkboxes \& Radio Buttons

*   **States:** Radio buttons have selected and unselected states. Checkboxes have checked, unchecked, and indeterminate (mixed) states.
*   **Target Area:** Touch target must be at least 48x48dp, even though the visual graphic is smaller.

### 6. Input Fields (Text Fields)

*   **Variants:** Filled and Outlined. Filled text fields use `{colors.sys-color-surface-container}` with a thick bottom stroke. Outlined text fields use a `{colors.sys-color-outline}` stroke and have a 10dp corner radius on iOS (4dp on Android). Both are 56dp tall (44dp on iOS).
*   **Error State:** When input is invalid, the stroke, label, and trailing error icon change to `{colors.sys-color-error}`. Replace standard helper text with error text to prevent elements from shifting down.
*   **Required Indicator:** Expose required fields with an asterisk (\*) appended to the label text.

### 7. AI Conversational Components

*   **Prompt Field:** The primary entry point for AI text, voice, and file inputs. Minimum height is 56dp. On desktop, the maximum height is 280dp (and scrolls); on mobile, it is 40% of screen height. Attachments are rendered as compact cards above the input. Submitting a prompt triggers a stop button and activates a processing indicator.
*   **Chat Bubble:** Displays submitted prompts. Maximum width is 708dp; max height is 320dp (otherwise collapsed to 5 lines with a truncate arrow). Sizing is responsive: Small screens use `{typography.body-medium}`, while Expanded windows use `{typography.body-large}`. Interactive icon buttons (Edit, Copy) appear below on Compact screens or on hover.
*   **Suggestions:** Single, binary, or multiple-choice suggestions stacked vertically on Compact screens, or side-by-side on Expanded screens. Maximum of 3 options. Minimum height is 48dp, with a corner radius of 28dp. Selecting a suggestion immediately submits it as a prompt.
*   **AI Outlined \& Icon Buttons:** Outlined buttons trigger AI processes, utilizing an AI system spark icon. During generation, they lose their outline and are filled with a rotating AI gradient. The text and icon must pass 4.5:1 contrast against the moving gradient by mapping to `{colors.sys-color-primary}`.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do's

*   **Do** maintain WCAG AA contrast standards (3:1 minimum for display/large text, 4.5:1 for normal/body/label text).
*   **Do** use the high-emphasis Filled button style for only the single most important action on a page.
*   **Do** ensure all touch target dimensions are at least 48x48dp (or 44x44pt on iOS) for accessibility, even when the visual icon or element is smaller.
*   **Do** display a visible focus indicator around active text fields and interactive elements upon keyboard navigation.
*   **Do** write button labels in sentence case, keeping them concise (1–3 words) and fully visible on a single line.
*   **Do** replace supporting text with error text in text fields to prevent layout shifts during validation errors.
*   **Do** underline hyperlinked text and color it using `{colors.sys-color-primary}` or `{colors.sys-color-tertiary}`.
*   **Do** align trailing action buttons in dialogs to the right (RTL automatically flips this to the left).

### Don'ts

*   **Don't** use more than three AI chips or prompt suggestions on a single screen to prevent cognitive overload.
*   **Don't** apply animated AI color gradients to buttons, chips, or inputs in their static resting state; reserve them strictly for active generating/processing states.
*   **Don't** apply the deprecated Surface Tint elevations (+1 to +5 overlays) or mix them with the new tone-based Surface Container roles.
*   **Don't** mix buttons with and without Material Glass effects in the same UI layer on iOS.
*   **Don't** use vague or generic terms like "Done", "OK", or "Close" for confirmation buttons in dialogs.
*   **Don't** place outlined or text buttons on busy, patterned, or visual backgrounds; use simple backdrops to preserve readability.
*   **Don't** apply Emphasized typography styles broadly across all body text; reserve them strictly for unread items, selected states, badges, and headers to maintain clear page hierarchy.
*   **Don't** allow text fields to span the full width of extra-large or desktop monitors; establish maximum width bounds to support comfortable reading.
